Data-Enabled Predictive Control for Adaptive V2X Resource Allocation

Graduate Research Engineer | UT Dallas

Aug 2024–Dec 2025

Vehicle-to-Everything (V2X) communication is critical for autonomous vehicle safety, but conventional fixed-parameter control schemes fail when traffic density changes dramatically. This research applied Data-Enabled Predictive Control (DeePC) — a model-free, data-driven control method — to dynamically allocate V2X communication resources (transmission power and beacon period) based on real-time traffic conditions. This was the first application of DeePC to V2X resource allocation.

Problem:

Fixed transmission power and beaconing intervals cause packet loss and channel saturation in high-density traffic, compromising collision avoidance and safety applications. Traditional model-based control (MPC) fails because V2X systems involve nonlinear dynamics, stochastic fading, and distributed multi-user interference that are nearly impossible to model analytically.

Approach:

  • Collected 715 training samples using LTE-V2V-Sim v5.4 with real NGSIM highway vehicle trajectories, systematically sweeping 10 power levels × 11 beacon period levels × 7 stochastic repetitions
  • Built Hankel matrices (35×709 dimensions) encoding temporal input-output relationships per Willems' Fundamental Lemma
  • Developed traffic-adaptive quadratic cost function with density-dependent reference schedules and weighting matrices
  • Implemented adaptive regularization that adjusts based on real-time prediction error
  • Validated through closed-loop simulation using 3GPP Release 14 Mode 4 protocol with Winner+ B1 channel model

Key Results:

  • 94% prediction accuracy (R² = 0.94 on PRR, 0.91 on CBR, 0.88 on Delay) on 624 held-out validation samples
  • Zero safety constraint violations across 150 test episodes (baseline failed 100% of the time)
  • 72% reduction in channel congestion (CBR: 80.4% → 22.6%)
  • 69% reduction in communication latency (197ms → 60ms)
  • 4× density extrapolation: trained on 50–100 vehicles/km, successfully controlled at 250–400 vehicles/km
  • 4.3% energy savings (22.01 dBm average vs. baseline 23 dBm fixed)

Tools & Technologies:

MATLAB/Simulink Python LTE-V2V-Sim v5.4 Quadratic Programming Hankel Matrices Statistical Analysis

Paper: Presented at ISME-2024, New Delhi. Full paper under review for journal publication.

Braking Dynamics of a Tadpole Type Three-Wheeler

Mechanical Design & Test Engineer | PES University

Jan–Apr 2023

Tadpole vehicles (two front wheels, one rear) are emerging in urban mobility but lack dedicated braking dynamics research. This project developed a modified mathematical model and 3-DOF Simulink simulation for straight-line braking dynamics, validated against empirical measurements from a physical testbed. The work filled a gap in existing literature by providing the first comprehensive braking dynamics study specific to tadpole configurations.

What I Did:

  • Co-led the full design, build, and test cycle for the braking system of a tadpole three-wheeler equipped with Alto disc brakes (Bosch tandem master cylinder, floating calipers, disc brakes on all 3 wheels)
  • Experimentally determined vehicle parameters: center of gravity height (432mm), static load distribution (2226.87N front, 1108.53N rear), and computed dynamic load transfer, brake forces, and brake torques
  • Performed complete brake system analysis: effective radius calculation, pedal ratio measurement, master cylinder pressure, actuation pressure, brake factor, back pressure, and full volumetric analysis (total loss 6.582 cm³ vs. master cylinder capacity 12.75 cm³ — confirming system operates within design limits)
  • Developed 3-DOF Simulink model (surge, heave, pitch) using Newton's laws with ode4 Runge-Kutta solver
  • Created detailed CAD models and engineering drawings in CATIA V5 and SolidWorks with GD&T per ASME Y14.5
  • Achieved 34% part count reduction (47 to 31 components) through design-for-manufacturing optimization

Simulation Results (at 60 km/h initial velocity):

  • Maximum deceleration: 5.4 m/s²
  • Braking distance: 27.1 m
  • Maximum pitch angle: 0.019 radians (1.08°)

Hydrogen Fuel Pressure Regulator Redesign

Team Member (Group of 4) | UT Dallas MECH 6303

Fall 2024

With the transition from gasoline to hydrogen fuel cell vehicles, conventional fuel pressure regulators require significant redesign to safely handle hydrogen gas. This project redesigned a fuel pressure regulator for hydrogen compatibility, addressing the unique challenges of hydrogen gas flow including higher pressures, leak potential, and material embrittlement.

Design Modifications:

  • Main body: added miniature ridges throughout intake, outlet, and return fuel pipes to prevent gas backflow; altered overall size for correct pressure delivery
  • Pressure cap: increased wall thickness to handle higher pressures from hydrogen gas flow
  • Coil spring: tightened stiffness for proper compression and airflow regulation
  • Diaphragm: changed material to prevent hydrogen gas leakage (gasoline liquid → hydrogen gas compatibility)
  • Seals and gaskets: modified geometry for tight fit with hydrogen-resistant materials
  • Pressure relief valve: redesigned to open at appropriate pressure threshold for hydrogen

Safety Features Incorporated:

  • Leak detection system for hydrogen gas
  • Pressure relief system for emergency venting of excess built-up pressure

Simulation Planning:

  • Stress analysis under operating loads
  • Pressure flow and fluid flow simulation
  • Thermal validation across 50–225°F operating range (engine-adjacent placement)
